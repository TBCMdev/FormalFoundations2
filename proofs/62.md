# $[x \equiv y \not\equiv ~z~ \equiv x \not\equiv y \equiv z]$

We observe for any $x, y, z$:

- Starting with the lhs, step 1 is almost always to get rid of the not equivalence:

    $$x \equiv y \not\equiv z \rightarrow x \equiv \neg(y \equiv z)$$

- Next we can use **(35)** to get rid of the brackets:
    $$x \equiv \neg(y \equiv z) \rightarrow x \equiv \neg y \equiv z$$
- We can flip around the $x$ and $\neg y$, to then plug into **(35)** to put the $\neg$ on the outside of the brackets:
    > Note the brackets arent necessary here but I believe you can add brackets anywhere in equivalences by just stating the rules.
    $$x \equiv \neg y \equiv z \rightarrow (\neg y \equiv x) \equiv z$$

- Finally we can convert this back into a not equivalence to get our answer:

    $$(\neg y \equiv x) \equiv z \rightarrow \neg (y \equiv x) \equiv z$$
    $$\rightarrow$$
    $$\neg (x \equiv y) \equiv z$$
    $$\rightarrow$$
    $$x \not\equiv y \equiv z$$
