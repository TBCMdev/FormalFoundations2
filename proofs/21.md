# $[x \land (y \equiv z \equiv w) \equiv x \land y \equiv x \land z \equiv x \land w]$

We observe for any $x, y ,z ,w$:

- Starting with the more unique **lhs**, It is almost identical to the **OR** rule of **$\lor / \equiv \equiv$ (8a)**. Lets try to convert the lhs to that by using the **Golden Rule (11)** with **$x, y := x, (y \equiv z \equiv w)$**:

    $$x \land (y \equiv z \equiv w) \rightarrow x \equiv y \equiv z \equiv w \equiv x \lor (y \equiv z \equiv w)$$

- Now we see the last part of our workings is in the form of **$\lor / \equiv \equiv$ (8a)**:

    $$x \lor (y \equiv z \equiv w) \rightarrow x \lor y \equiv x \lor z \equiv x \lor w$$
    So we have:
    $$x \equiv y \equiv z \equiv w \equiv x \lor y \equiv x \lor z \equiv x \lor w$$

- Lets now try to convert our $x \lor y$, $x \lor z$, and $x \lor w$ to their and equivalents using the **Golden Rule (11)**:

    $$x \lor y \rightarrow x \equiv y \equiv x \land y$$ $$x \lor z \rightarrow x \equiv z \equiv x \land z$$ $$x \lor w \rightarrow x \equiv w \equiv x \land w$$

    If we insert them all together (not necessary and probably easier otherwise), we get:

    $$x \equiv y \equiv z \equiv w \equiv x \equiv y \equiv x \land y \equiv x \equiv z \equiv x \land z \equiv x \equiv w \equiv x \land w$$

- By the law of **symmetry (1)**, if we have two of the same equivalences equaling **something** we can just get rid of them and have that **something** left:

    $$x \equiv y \equiv y \rightarrow x$$
    
    $$x \equiv z \equiv w \equiv x \equiv x \land y \equiv x \equiv z \equiv x \land z \equiv x \equiv w \equiv x \land w$$
    
    Again with everything else:
    $$x \equiv z \equiv z \rightarrow x$$ $$x \equiv w \equiv w \rightarrow x$$

    We are left with:
    $$x \land y \equiv x \equiv x \equiv x \land z \equiv x \equiv x \equiv x \land w$$

- Finally we use the same law to cross out the four $\equiv x$ instances:

    $$x \equiv x \equiv x \rightarrow x$$ $$\rightarrow$$

    $$x \land y \equiv x \equiv x \equiv x \land z \equiv x \land w$$

    And:

    $$x \land y \equiv x \equiv x \rightarrow x \land y$$ $$\rightarrow$$

    $$x \land y \equiv x \land z \equiv x \land w$$
