# $[x \land (y \land z) \equiv (x \land y) \land z]$

We observe for any $x, y , z$:

- Starting with the lhs $x \land (y \land z)$, using **Golden Rule (11)** with **$x, y := x, (y \land z)$**:

    $$x \land (y \land z) \rightarrow x \equiv (y \land z) \equiv x \lor (y \land z)$$

- Then observing that there is 2 instances of **$y \land z$**, we can use the **Golden Rule (11)** again to replace them both:

    $$y \land z \rightarrow y \equiv z \equiv y \lor z = a$$

    $$\therefore x \equiv a \equiv x \lor a$$ $$\rightarrow$$ $$x \equiv y \equiv z \equiv y \lor z \equiv x \lor (y \equiv z \equiv y \lor z)$$

- Next we can see a $x \lor (y \equiv z \equiv w)$ pattern with **$x \lor (y \equiv z \equiv y \lor z)$**, so we can use **$\lor /\equiv$ (8a)** with **$w = (y \lor z)$**:

    $$x \lor (y \equiv z \equiv y \lor z) \rightarrow x \lor y \equiv x \lor z \equiv x \lor (y \lor z)$$ $$\rightarrow$$ $$x \equiv y \equiv z \equiv y \lor z \equiv x \lor  y \equiv x \lor z \equiv x \lor (y \lor z)$$


- By the law of **symmetry (1)**, we can move around any equivalence. We can see there is a mixed up partial of the **Golden Rule (11)** in our workings:
    $$x \equiv y \equiv x \lor y \rightarrow x \land y$$
    So we can move around our equivalences and replace it:
    $$x \equiv y \equiv x \lor y \equiv z \equiv y \lor z \equiv  x \lor z \equiv x \lor (y \lor z)$$ $$\rightarrow$$ $$x \equiv y \equiv x \lor y \equiv z \equiv y \lor z \equiv  x \lor z \equiv x \lor (y \lor z)$$ $$\rightarrow$$ $$x \land y \equiv z \equiv y \lor z \equiv  x \lor z \equiv x \lor (y \lor z)$$

- We can see three **OR** operations going on, $y \lor z$ & $x \lor z$ & $x \lor (y \lor z)$.
    Due to **Associativity (6)**, we can reorder the parenthesis (brackets) of a triple OR operation. If we reorder our last operation, and swap the first two around using symmetry, we can get $\bold z$ being OR'ED 3 times, and can thus use the **$\lor / \equiv$ (8a)** theorem:

    $$y \lor z \rightarrow z \lor y$$ $$x \lor z \rightarrow z \lor x$$ $$x \lor (y \lor z) \rightarrow z \lor (x \lor y)$$
    Using **8a** with **$x = z, y = y, z = x, w = x \lor y$**:
    $$ z \lor y \equiv z \lor x \equiv z \lor (x \lor y) \rightarrow z \lor (y \equiv x \equiv x \lor y)$$

    Now we can substitute that in for our three OR operations:
    $$x \land y \equiv z \equiv z \lor (y \equiv x \equiv x \lor y)$$

- The **Golden Rule (11)** can simplify **$(y \equiv x \equiv x \lor y)$**:
    $$y \equiv x \equiv x \lor y \rightarrow x \land y$$
    So we have:
    $$x \land y \equiv z \equiv z \lor (x \land y)$$

- Finally, Our entire equation is 34 of the **Golden Rule (11)** with **$x = (x \land y), y = z$**:

    $$\rightarrow (x \land y) \land z$$
