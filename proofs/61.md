# $[(x \not\equiv y) \equiv z \equiv (x \not\equiv (y \equiv z))]]$

We observe for any $x, y, z$:

> For proofs with $\not\equiv$, its best to get rid of them asap using **(40)** or otherwise.

- We start with the lhs and get rid of the not equivalence:

    $$(x \not\equiv y) \equiv z \rightarrow \neg(x \equiv y) \equiv z$$

- Now notice the conversion looks similar to **(37)**:

    ```math
    \neg(x \equiv y) \equiv z \\
    (37) \rightarrow \neg x \equiv y
    ```
    So we use **(37)** with $x, y := (x \equiv y), y$:
    ```math
    \neg(x \equiv y) \equiv z \rightarrow (x \equiv y) \equiv \neg z
    ```
- Since the answer is looking for the form $x \not\equiv (...)$, we need to move that bracket away from the x. We can do that using our laws of **associativity (0)**:
    $$\rightarrow \\ x \equiv (y \equiv \neg z)$$

- Finally, our rhs is in the form of **$\neg over \equiv $ (35)**:
    $$\rightarrow \\ x \equiv \neg (y \equiv z)$$
- And since the negation of equivalence is the same thing as its symbol ($\not\equiv$) in rule **(59)** we get:
    $$x \not\equiv (y \equiv z)$$
